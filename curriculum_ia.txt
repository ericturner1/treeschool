Use Grade 1 Math as a tree, not a flat list. Based on the topic structure on IXL's grade-1 math page, the right shape is:

- numbers and counting
- comparison
- addition
- subtraction
- mixed add/subtract
- place value / numbers to 100 or 120
- skip counting / equal groups
- word problems
- measurement / geometry / time / money later

Source used:
- https://www.ixl.com/math/grade-1

Recommendation
Model curriculum in 6 layers:

1. Program
- K-12

2. Grade
- 1st Grade

3. Subject
- Math

4. Domain
- big buckets like Numbers to 100, Addition to 20, Subtraction to 20, Word Problems

5. Cluster
- smaller instructional groups inside a domain
- example: Understand Addition, Addition Strategies to 10, Addition Facts to 10

6. Skill or Lesson Target
- atomic teachable/checkable item
- example: Add with pictures to 10

That gives you stable IA for both curriculum authoring and student progress.

Core entities
I'd define these conceptually:

- CurriculumNode
  - generic tree node
  - type: grade | subject | domain | cluster | skill
  - title
  - slug
  - description
  - parent
  - order

- Skill
  - mastery unit
  - short student-facing title
  - internal learning objective
  - difficulty
  - estimated minutes
  - mastery threshold
  - prerequisite skills

- Lesson
  - one teaching experience for a skill
  - warmup
  - instruction
  - guided practice
  - independent practice
  - extension
  - offline activity

- AssessmentItem
  - question/task
  - item type: multiple_choice | numeric | drag_drop | manipulative | word_problem
  - prompt
  - answer key
  - hints
  - explanation

- SkillProgress
  - per student, per skill
  - status: not_started | in_progress | mastered | needs_review
  - attempts
  - accuracy
  - last practiced

- StandardAlignment
  - map a skill to Common Core or your own treesckool standard
  - important if you want a traditional US-quality education claim

- Resource
  - printable
  - manipulatives list
  - parent guide
  - offline homestead extension

For 1st grade math specifically
Start with these domains:

1. Counting and Number Sense
- count to 20
- count to 100
- count to 120
- forward/backward counting
- number lines
- number charts
- even/odd
- ordinal numbers

2. Place Value and Number Structure
- tens and ones
- compare two-digit numbers
- compose/decompose numbers
- read/write numbers

3. Comparison
- more/fewer
- greater/less/equal
- compare sets
- compare numbers with symbols

4. Addition
- understand addition with objects/pictures
- addition sentences
- number lines
- counting on
- make ten
- doubles
- facts to 10
- facts to 20

5. Subtraction
- understand subtraction with objects/pictures
- subtraction sentences
- counting back
- counting on
- use addition to subtract
- facts to 10
- facts to 20

6. Mixed Operations and Word Problems
- choose add vs subtract
- fact families
- unknown addend
- one-step word problems
- represent problems with pictures/equations

7. Skip Counting and Foundations for Multiplicative Thinking
- count by 2s, 5s, 10s
- equal groups
- repeated addition
- simple arrays

I would not start with measurement, money, time, and geometry until the math core structure is stable.

What a single skill should contain
For example: Add with pictures to 10

- title
- objective
- student outcome
- prerequisites
- instruction model
- practice item bank
- mastery rule
- common mistakes
- parent/offline extension
- localization-ready copy

That last point matters because content may exist in arbitrary languages.

Localization-aware structure
Do not put display text directly in the structural entity.

Use:
- stable structural IDs
- localized content per locale

Example conceptually:
- skill id: math.g1.addition.understand.add-with-pictures-10
- English content
- Spanish content
- Arabic content

So structure stays fixed; only text/media change by locale.

Support structures you will need
Beyond curriculum itself:

- PrerequisiteGraph
  - not every skill is just parent/child
  - example: make-ten supports both addition and subtraction fluency

- MasteryPolicy
  - define what mastered means
  - example: 3 sessions, 90 percent accuracy, no hint dependence

- PlacementEntryPoint
  - lets a child start at the right cluster instead of lesson 1

- PacingGuide
  - recommended weekly sequence for parents

- OfflineExtension
  - important for treesckool
  - example: count eggs, measure seed rows, compare harvest quantities

- ParentNotes
  - what to watch for
  - when to slow down
  - enrichment ideas

What I would not do yet
Do not start by modeling:
- units, lessons, quizzes, standards, games, and printable packs as separate top-level silos
- that becomes messy fast

Start with:
- curriculum tree
- skill
- lesson
- assessment item
- progress
- localization content
- standards mapping

That is enough for a real MVP.

Recommended first-grade math v1 scope
For an MVP, I'd launch only:

1. Counting and number sense
2. Comparing numbers
3. Addition to 10
4. Subtraction to 10
5. Mixed word problems to 10

Then add:
6. Addition/subtraction to 20
7. Place value
8. Skip counting/equal groups

That gives you a coherent first-grade experience without overbuilding.

Key product decision
Decide now whether your atomic tracked unit is:

- lesson
or
- skill

I recommend skill.

Reason:
- lessons can vary by language, medium, and pedagogy
- skills are the stable mastery object
- student progress, placement, recommendations, and reporting work better on skills

So:
- skill is the tracked entity
- lesson is one way to teach that skill
